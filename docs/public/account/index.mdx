# Your Proctor account

Your Proctor <Term id="user">User</Term> is one account in one Institution.
A password, a sign-in provider and MFA are all ways to prove that the account
is yours. They are not separate accounts. On their own, they don't make you a
member of anything or give you any permissions.

## Signing in doesn't give you access on its own

When you sign in, Proctor learns who you are, and how recently and how
strongly you proved it. Each time you do something, Proctor still checks
whether you are allowed to do that exact thing right now. Your
<Term id="affiliation">Affiliation</Term>, your Academic Unit membership, your
Class membership and any <Term id="role-binding">Role Binding</Term> are
separate records, and each one lasts until it is changed.

This explains two things people often notice:

- You can sign in without any problem and still be unable to open an Exam or
  manage a Class.
- When a membership or Role Binding ends, you can lose access, but your
  account and your ways of signing in stay as they were.

## Ask for help safely

When you contact an administrator, include the time the action failed, the
server address, the error code shown, and the request ID if there is one.
Never send a password, cookie, bearer credential, DPoP proof or nonce,
Invitation claim, recovery code or Attempt credential.

While you are signed out, Proctor sometimes shows a general error on purpose.
A more specific message would reveal whether an account exists.
