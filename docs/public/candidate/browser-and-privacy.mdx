# The Exam browser and your privacy

When the Exam's selected Revision turns on Browser Policy, Desktop gives you
one Exam browser. Its allowlist decides which pages you can open at the top
level. It can match on HTTPS address, host, start of the path, redirects, and
a rule for which page you start on. It doesn't turn general web browsing into
part of the Exam.

## What can be recorded

Browser Activity covers opening and closing the browser, top-level navigation
or redirects that were allowed, and top-level navigation that was blocked.
Each event that is kept has:

- details the server attaches, about your Participation, the event's source
  and its sequence number;
- the policy Revision in force at the time;
- the time on your computer;
- one reason, from a fixed list;
- a cut-down version of the address.

How much of the address is kept depends on what happened:

- For allowed HTTPS navigation, Proctor may keep the host in its standard
  form, the port if it isn't the default one, and the path.
- For blocked HTTP, it may keep the same parts, with the scheme `http`.
- For any other scheme that isn't allowed, it keeps only the scheme, in lower
  case.
- For an invalid URL, it keeps an empty scheme, host and path.

## What Proctor never collects

Query strings, fragments, user names or passwords in URLs, page content, page
titles, referrers, headers, cookies, page structure (DOM data), downloads,
local file paths and anything carried inside a scheme are not part of the
policy and are not recorded. The first version of integrity evidence also
leaves out screenshots, webcam, clipboard, source code and any other
open-ended data.

Activity is evidence kept to a minimum to protect your privacy. It isn't a
verdict. Neither the time on your computer nor anything Desktop claims decides
how serious something is, or whether you did anything wrong. If records are
missing or have gaps, Proctor marks that uncertainty openly. The server never
invents navigation that didn't happen.

Desktop may keep sending records while the Sitting is paused, or while a
correction is waiting for you to acknowledge it. That lets the records catch
up, but it doesn't let you interact with the Exam.

## Who can see it and how long it is kept

Your ordinary browser history is visible only to the Exam's appointed Managers
who also have the Browser Activity permission. A general administrator
override doesn't give anyone access to this history. Some blocked navigation
may also create a separate, cut-down copy as integrity evidence, for
authorized Review.

The server gives you a current collection notice. It shows your Institution's
retention period for browser records, and whether the policy may create
integrity evidence. A retention period of zero days means no expiry has been
set.

The retention clock depends on the records for the closed Sitting being
complete in a way that counts as eligible. Preservation holds and incomplete records can
mean records are kept longer. Integrity evidence has its own retention period,
so your ordinary history may expire while a copy kept for Review remains. The
notice tells you what is collected. It isn't asking you to grant a new
permission or give consent.
