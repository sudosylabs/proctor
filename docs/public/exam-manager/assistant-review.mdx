# Read assistant use in review

When your Exam offers the assistant, every Submission carries the full
record of how the candidate used it. You read it during review, next to the
sealed work and the integrity record described in
[look at a Submission and review integrity](./submission-and-integrity-review.mdx).

## What is recorded

The conversation is its own append-only record, owned by the Attempt:

- every turn, from the candidate and from the assistant, with its text;
- for each assistant turn, the model, the token counts and why it stopped;
- every tool call: its name, its arguments, a shortened result, how long it
  took and how it ended;
- every approval request and the candidate's answer;
- every output check, and whether a reply was rewritten or replaced;
- every gap, such as a provider outage or a turn cancelled at the seal.

Changes the assistant makes to files appear in the workspace journal under
the assistant as their author, linked to the exact tool call. Commands it
runs appear as processes under their own `assistant:N` tag, with their exit
code and output.

The record has its own limits, apart from the execution evidence, so a long
conversation never pushes terminal evidence out, and the other way round. It
is bound to the Submission when the Attempt is sealed, and the receipt lists
its digest.

## Read it
The transcript is read with
`GET /api/v1/submissions/{submission_id}/assistant-transcript`, one page at a
time, and only after the Attempt is sealed. It is authorized like the
Submission's attempt evidence: you need to manage the Exam and hold review
permission.
Use these API records together when reviewing:

- **The timeline:** compare transcript timestamps with the workspace journal
  and execution evidence to see what the candidate did before and after
  each answer.
- **Per file:** which lines the assistant last wrote, from the manifest's
  last author and the journal.
- **Per command:** what the assistant ran, its exit code and its output.
- **The usage summary:** turns, tokens, tools used, commands, edits proposed,
  accepted and declined, and output checks.

At Pair, a proposed diff the candidate applied is the candidate's own edit
in the journal. The transcript records the proposal and whether it was
applied.

## Facts, not flags

When your Exam allows the assistant, using it isn't misconduct. Proctor
raises no Integrity Flags for assistant use, and the usage summary has no
score, threshold or rating. A high or low figure means nothing on its own.

Judge the record as you would any other evidence: what the Exam allowed,
what the candidate asked, and what they did with the answers. Don't treat
the transcript as proof of what the candidate understood.

## Candidates see their own transcript

The candidate can read their own conversation during the Attempt, and the
receipt lists its digest. Nothing about their assistant use is hidden from
them. Your instructions to the assistant are not part of what they see, but
assume they may have learned them from the model.
