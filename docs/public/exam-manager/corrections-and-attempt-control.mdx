# Correct a live Exam and control Attempts

You can send an urgent correction to an open or paused Sitting. The Sitting
stays the same, and candidates don't have to join again. The correction
creates another Revision that never changes, and points only that Sitting at
it.

<GovernedFigure asset="correction-acknowledgement-sequence" />

## What a correction can change

A live correction can change the instructions, the ordered Exam Resources and
the Browser Policy. It can't change the Exam Policy, the Starter Workspace,
which Class is eligible, or the schedule. Proctor checks that the digests of
those protected parts still match the current Revision.

To add or replace a Resource's file, you first stage it. Each stage is tied to
the exact Sitting, the base Revision, the kind of target, you as the Manager,
and an expiry time. Candidates can't see anything you stage.

The correction itself then sends one complete, ordered list of Resources:

- leaving a Resource out removes it;
- its place in the list becomes its new position;
- an item with no stage keeps its content from the base Revision;
- an item with a ready stage uses the staged bytes.

Stages that fail or that you abandon stay invisible, and become eligible to be
cleaned up under retention.

## Write a notice candidates can safely read

Every correction has:

- a summary of 1–500 characters, with spaces trimmed from each end;
- the list of areas that changed, in a standard order;
- a clear yes or no on whether candidates must acknowledge it.

Keep your private reason for the change separate. The candidate notice should
explain what changed without revealing how policy is evaluated internally,
mentioning another candidate, or accusing anyone.

When acknowledgement is required, each Attempt must acknowledge notices in
order, oldest first. While an acknowledgement is waiting, the candidate can't
change their Workspace, navigate in the Exam browser or submit. They can
still:

- read protected content;
- renew their Participation;
- send Focus Loss and Browser Activity records;
- send the acknowledgement itself;
- have their Attempt closed by someone authorized.

The pending acknowledgement belongs to the Attempt. It stays in place if the
candidate reconnects or is allowed back in.

## Know which control does what

| Control | What it means | What it keeps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kick | an authorized person stops the active Attempt by hand | the Attempt, its Workspace, its evidence, and a separate record that this was manual |
| automatic Suspension | the policy, or a confirmed loss of connection, stops the Attempt | neutral evidence, the Flag, the episode that triggered it, and the Workspace |
| re-allow | closes one exact Suspension episode and puts the Attempt back to Ready | Flags, evidence, the fixed configuration and earlier generations |
| manager end | seals any unfinished Ready, Active or Suspended Attempt | the last Workspace the server confirmed, with `manager_ended_attempt` recorded on the Submission |
| Sitting close | seals every unfinished Attempt that was created, at the deadline or when you close early | `sitting_closed` recorded on the Submission, plus any earlier continuity cause |

To Kick a candidate, you give the expected Attempt revision and a short
private reason. Kick immediately ends the candidate's live generation and
Connection. It doesn't create an Integrity Flag. The candidate sees only that
a manager suspended the Attempt.

To re-allow, you give the exact Suspension, the expected Attempt revision and
a short private reason. When the candidate enters again, Proctor starts a new
Participation generation, and old credentials can't bring the old one back.
Only the window that triggered the Suspension is reset, so later violations
can still lead to enforcement.

Ending an Attempt yourself is final. It never reopens an Attempt or submits it
again. Your private reason never appears in anything the candidate sees, in
emails, in realtime updates or on the Submission. At or after the deadline,
closing the Sitting does the same sealing instead.
