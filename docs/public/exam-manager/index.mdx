# Manage Exams

As an <Term id="exam-manager">Exam Manager</Term>, you share responsibility for
one Exam. To act on it in the normal way, you need two things: a current
Manager relationship with the Exam, and a Role permission that applies at the
Exam's Academic Unit. Who created the Exam, who owns it now, who manages it and
who has permission are four separate facts.

## The lifecycle, step by step

1. [Write and publish](./author-and-publish.mdx) your editable Draft as a
   numbered Revision that never changes.
2. [Add protected Resources and a Starter Workspace](./resources-and-workspace.mdx).
   Every Attempt receives them, and only through the protected application.
3. Schedule and run an Exam Sitting for one exact Revision.
4. [Look at finished Submissions and review integrity](./submission-and-integrity-review.mdx),
   separately from academic grading.
5. [Finalize the Review and release the result](./finalize-and-release.mdx),
   which shows candidates only a short result.

## Ownership and management

The person who creates an Exam is recorded as its creator for good, and starts
as its owner and a Manager. An Exam always has exactly one current owner, and
Proctor protects that.

- Adding a Manager gives them the same management responsibility as you, but
  doesn't make them the owner.
- Transferring ownership leaves the previous owner as a Manager.
- Before you remove a Manager, transfer ownership first if they are the owner.

A new Manager must be an active User who is currently a member of the Exam's
exact Academic Unit. The server checks this again while it makes the change,
with the Exam locked and its revision checked. If someone loses their
membership or Role permission, they lose normal access, but the record of who
created the Exam and who was given access stays.

A system administrator override is a deliberate action, and it is recorded in
the audit history. It doesn't make the administrator a Manager. It also
doesn't get around the rules for archiving, ownership, lineage, revisions or
published content that can't change.

## What Proctor does and doesn't do

Proctor delivers programming Exams securely and supports integrity review. It
doesn't define scores, rubrics, grading, pass or fail, or academic outcomes.
Don't put those meanings into a Draft policy or a Submission Review.
