# Look at a Submission and review integrity

A Submission tells you which confirmed work was sealed, when, and how the
Attempt ended. An integrity review is about how you, as a manager, should read
the limited record Proctor kept. Neither one is a grade or a finding of
misconduct. Neither gives you permission to piece together what else the
candidate was doing on their device.

## Start with how the Attempt ended

Every Attempt that was created ends with at most one Submission, which never
changes. Its provenance tells you how it ended:

- `candidate_submitted` means the candidate finished the in-sync submit
  process themselves;
- `manager_ended_attempt` means an authorized Manager chose to seal the latest
  confirmed work before the Sitting deadline;
- `sitting_closed` means the Sitting closed, on schedule or early, and sealed
  the unfinished Attempts that had been created.

<GovernedFigure asset="submission-provenance-boundary" />

Provenance only says how the Attempt ended. It says nothing about the quality
of the work or what the candidate intended. If a candidate never entered,
Proctor doesn't create an Attempt or Submission for them. If a manager ended
the Attempt, their private reason stays private, even though the candidate's
receipt names how the Attempt ended.

## Look only at the sealed record

The Submission header records:

- the Exam Revision that applied;
- the confirmed Workspace Cursor;
- the final integrity state;
- the number of unresolved discrepancies;
- the provenance;
- the submission time.

Its manifest, the list of submitted files, keeps each entry's fixed identity,
its path, where its content came from, its version, checksum, media type and
size.

You can page through the manifest and open the stored file contents, but only
after the Attempt is sealed. You can't read a candidate's Workspace while they
are working. Don't treat storage keys or local Desktop paths as the shape of
the Submission. The manifest, which never changes, is what counts.

## Read the integrity record in order

<GovernedFigure asset="integrity-review-release-boundary" />

Native condition records keep every change in a native rule's status, as the
server worked it out from what the candidate's Desktop reported: which rule,
why, when, and whether the change began or ended a stretch when the rule wasn't
met. When a rule the Exam enforces stops being met, Proctor raises a Flag for
that capability, such as a second display, with one piece of evidence each time
it happens. Rules that only observe, and stretches where Proctor couldn't tell,
never raise one.

When the Exam records the screen, a Flag also asks the candidate's Desktop for
the minute either side of it. You can ask for other ranges yourself, with a
short private reason, up to ten per Attempt. Each request and each playback is
audited.

Look at these together:

- **Flags** group the evidence that met a policy rule. Browser Flags also
  record the Participation, the fixed policy Revision and the rule.
- **Evidence** keeps a limited number of qualifying episodes, with a summary
  of any beyond that.
- **Native conditions** keep each rule status change. When you finalize the
  Review, you acknowledge the current list of them. Native Flags need a
  decision like any other Flag.
- **Screen clips** are the recorded ranges asked for around Flags or by a
  manager. A clip still uploading after the deadline shows as failed.
- **Discrepancies** keep gaps, missing acknowledgements of corrections,
  incomplete Browser Activity, late Focus Loss and other uncertainty, in the
  open.
- **Submission integrity state** is `settled` only when collection finished
  with no unresolved gaps. `gapped` means some uncertainty remains, and it
  stays visible.

The server doesn't rely on the candidate's clock, on claims about how serious
something was, or on a manager's hunch. Connection Loss is based on a
confirmed lease expiry. One failed request or ping isn't enough. Browser
Activity is the cut-down record of top-level pages described in
[The Exam browser and your privacy](../candidate/browser-and-privacy.mdx). It never
includes page content or general browsing history.

## Record a decision, not a verdict

Before you can finalize, every Flag needs one current decision: `confirmed`,
`dismissed` or `inconclusive`. Use `inconclusive` when the record can't
support a reliable conclusion. Don't force uncertainty into one of the other
two.

Your rationale is seen only by managers. It must explain the reason for your
decision, based on the evidence. The candidate never sees it. Private Review
notes are also kept apart from the optional Markdown remarks you approve for
the student.

When another manager makes a change at the same time, the Review revision
moves on. If your revision is out of date, read the Review again and work
their changes in. Don't guess the next number or write over their work.

After a source has closed, new qualifying Browser evidence or native
conditions can still arrive during its limited upload window. When they do,
the current Review is no longer approved, and Records Completion is out of
date. Earlier finalized snapshots stay available. Look at the new records,
update the affected Browser Flag decisions, then finalize and release again.
A delivery that is an exact repeat doesn't reopen a Review.
