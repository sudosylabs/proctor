# Inspect a Submission and Review Integrity

A Submission answers “what acknowledged work was sealed, when, and by which
terminal path?” Integrity review answers “how should a manager interpret the
bounded record?” Neither object is a grade, a misconduct verdict, or permission
to reconstruct a candidate's broader device activity.

## Begin With Submission Provenance

Every created Attempt reaches at most one immutable Submission:

- `candidate_submitted` means the candidate completed the synchronized
  voluntary-submission handshake;
- `manager_ended_attempt` means an authorized Manager deliberately sealed the
  latest acknowledged state before the Sitting deadline; and
- `sitting_closed` means scheduled or early Sitting closure sealed unfinished
  created Attempts.

<GovernedFigure asset="submission-provenance-boundary" />

Provenance describes the terminal path, not quality or intent. A no-show has no
fabricated Attempt or Submission. A manager-ending private reason remains
private even though the candidate-safe receipt names the terminal provenance.

## Inspect Only the Sealed Record

The Submission header pins the governing Exam Revision, acknowledged Workspace
Cursor, integrity terminal state, unresolved discrepancy count, provenance,
and submission time. Its manifest preserves stable entry identities, logical
paths, content origins, versions, checksums, media types, and sizes.

Managers can page the manifest and open retained file bytes only after sealing.
They cannot read a live candidate Workspace. Do not treat object-storage keys,
local Desktop paths, as the Submission hierarchy;
the immutable manifest is authority.

## Read the Integrity Chain in Order

<GovernedFigure asset="integrity-review-release-boundary" />

Native condition records keep every change of a native rule's status, as the
server worked it out from what the candidate's Desktop reported: which rule,
why, when, and whether it began or ended a stretch of time the rule was not met.
When a rule the Exam enforces stops being met, Proctor raises a Flag for that
capability, such as a second display, with one piece of evidence each time it
happens. Rules that only observe, and stretches where Proctor could not tell,
never raise one. Operational source health is separate from the records
available for Review.

When the Exam records the screen, a Flag also asks the candidate's Desktop for
the minute either side of it. You can ask for other ranges yourself, with a
short private reason, up to ten per Attempt. Asking and each playback are
audited.

Review these sources together:

- **Flags** group qualifying policy evidence. Browser Flags also distinguish
  Participation, frozen policy Revision and rule.
- **Evidence** retains bounded qualifying episodes and summarized overflow.
- **Native conditions** keep each rule status change; Review finalization
  acknowledges their current inventory. Native Flags need a decision like any
  other Flag.
- **Screen clips** are the recorded ranges asked for around Flags or by a
  manager. A clip still uploading after the deadline shows as failed.
- **Discrepancies** retain gaps, missing correction acknowledgement, incomplete
  Browser Activity, late Focus Loss, and other explicit uncertainty.
- **Submission integrity state** is `settled` only when collection completed
  without unresolved gaps; `gapped` means the uncertainty remains visible.

Client clocks, severity claims, and manager intuition are not server authority.
Connection Loss is based on confirmed lease expiry, while one failed request or
ping is not enough. Browser Activity is the minimized top-level record described
in [Browser and Privacy](../candidate/browser-and-privacy.mdx), never page
content or general browsing history.

## Record a Disposition, Not a Verdict

Every Flag requires one current `confirmed`, `dismissed`, or `inconclusive`
decision before finalization. Use `inconclusive` when the retained record cannot
support a reliable conclusion; do not force uncertainty into either of the
other outcomes.

The rationale is manager-only and must explain the evidence-based reason for
the disposition. It is not shown to the candidate. Private Review notes are
also separate from optional approved student-facing Markdown remarks.

Concurrent manager changes advance the Review revision. If the revision is
stale, reread the Review and reconcile the other manager's work rather than
guessing the next number or overwriting it.

New qualifying Browser evidence or native conditions can arrive during a closed
source's bounded upload window. They withdraw approval of the current Review and
make Records Completion stale. Previously finalized snapshots remain available;
inspect the new inventory, refresh affected Browser Flag decisions, finalize, and
explicitly release again. An exact delivery replay does not reopen a Review.
