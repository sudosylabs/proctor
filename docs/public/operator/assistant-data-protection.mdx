# Assistant data protection

Turning the assistant on sends candidates' exam work to a model provider.
This page sets out what is sent, what Proctor does to limit it, and the legal
frameworks institutions usually need to consider. It is not legal advice.
Your institution's data protection officer or legal counsel decides what
applies to you.

## What the provider receives

For each turn, Proctor sends the provider:

- the system prompt, which includes the Exam Manager's instructions and the
  concepts the assistant should avoid;
- the conversation so far: the candidate's messages and the assistant's
  replies;
- at Reviewer and above, what the assistant's tools return: the contents of
  workspace files and visible text Exam Resources, search results, the screen of a candidate terminal, and
  the output of commands the assistant ran.

The provider never receives other candidates' work, or anything outside the
one Attempt. The assistant can't read more than the candidate can.

## What stays in Proctor

- The full transcript: every turn, tool call, approval and output check. It
  is stored in PostgreSQL with the Attempt, and bound to the Submission when
  the Attempt is sealed.
- Provider API keys, which live only in configuration.
- Structured candidate identity such as the user ID and account details.

The transcript is integrity evidence. It is deleted when the Submission's
integrity evidence is retired under the retention policy, and it follows the
same backup rules as the rest of the Submission.

## Pseudonymization

Profiles default `pseudonymize` to true. Proctor omits structured candidate
identity for every profile, including profiles where the setting is false.
The prompt refers to "the candidate"; it sends no account or Attempt ID.

Pseudonymization hides what Proctor knows about the candidate. It can't hide
what the candidate writes. A name in a code comment, a student number in a
file, or personal details typed into the chat all reach the provider as they
are. Tell candidates so; the [candidate guide](../candidate/assistant.mdx)
does.

## Retention depends on the provider

Each assistant profile records its provider's retention terms in
`data_handling`. The value is shown to Exam Managers when they choose a
profile, so they know what they are agreeing to. Proctor never sends it to
the provider, and it doesn't enforce anything by itself: it records the
agreement you have in place.

| `data_handling` | Use it when |
| --- | --- |
| `self_hosted` | The model runs on servers your institution controls, and no third party receives the prompts. |
| `zero_retention` | A hosted vendor has agreed in writing not to keep prompts or replies after answering. |
| `standard_30d` | A hosted vendor keeps API data for up to 30 days by default, for example for abuse monitoring, then deletes it. |
| `other` | Any other arrangement. Describe it in the profile's display name or your own records. |

Check each vendor's current terms rather than relying on a default you read
once: terms change, and some vendors keep data longer for flagged content.
Make sure the vendor doesn't use API data to train models.

## Frameworks to consider

### FERPA, in the United States

Candidates' exam work is usually part of their education records. Under the
[FERPA school-official exception](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/faq/who-school-official-under-ferpa) (34 CFR 99.31(a)(1)), an institution may
disclose education records to a contractor without consent when the
contractor:

- performs a service the institution would otherwise use its own staff for;
- has a legitimate educational interest under the institution's annual
  notice;
- is under the institution's direct control regarding the use and
  maintenance of the records;
- uses the records only for the authorized purpose and doesn't redisclose
  them.

A written agreement is the Department of Education's
[recommended way to establish direct control](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/faq/must-school-have-written-agreement-or-contract-community-based-organization-which-it-non).
Confirm that your arrangement meets the exception; FERPA does not itself
make a written contract mandatory for this exception.

### GDPR, in the EU and the UK

The institution is the controller. A hosted vendor that processes
candidates' work on its behalf is a processor, so you need a contract that
meets [GDPR Article 28](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng): processing only on your instructions,
confidentiality, security, approved sub-processors, help with data subjects'
rights, and deletion or return at the end. If the vendor processes data
outside the EU or the UK, you also need a lawful transfer mechanism under
Chapter V.

A data protection impact assessment under [Article 35](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng) is likely to be
required. The assistant uses a new technology, processes students' work
systematically in an assessment, and may involve a third party. Do the DPIA
before the first live Exam, and record the profile settings it relies on.

### Other laws

Other jurisdictions have their own student privacy and AI rules. Proctor's
assistant output is descriptive: it raises no Integrity Flags and makes no
decision about a candidate, and a person judges the record. Confirm with
your advisers how your local rules treat the assistant itself.

## A checklist before the first live Exam

1. Decide self-hosted or hosted, and why.
2. For a hosted vendor: sign the processor or school-official agreement,
   confirm retention and no training, and check transfers.
3. Complete the DPIA, or record why one isn't needed.
4. Create assistant profiles with `pseudonymize` on and the correct
   `data_handling`.
5. Tell candidates, before the Exam, that the assistant sends their work to
   a model and that the conversation is recorded.
